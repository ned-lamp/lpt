1. INTRODUCTION
---------------
Blockchain  : a shared, distributed digital ledger where data is stored in
              blocks linked together using cryptographic hashes.
Block       : contains data (transactions), its own hash and the previous block's hash.
Decentralized : no single owner; many computers (nodes) keep a copy.
Immutable   : once recorded, data cannot be changed (changing one block breaks the chain).
Consensus   : the method nodes use to agree on the valid chain
              (Proof of Work, Proof of Stake, PBFT etc.).

2. TYPES OF BLOCKCHAIN
----------------------
a) Public Blockchain (Permissionless)
   - Anyone can join, read, write and validate.
   - Fully decentralized and transparent.
   - Examples: Bitcoin, Ethereum.
   - Advantages   : transparent, secure, no central control, trustless.
   - Disadvantages: slow, high energy use (PoW), high fees, less privacy.

b) Private Blockchain (Permissioned)
   - Controlled by one organization; only invited members can join.
   - Examples: Hyperledger Fabric, R3 Corda (single-company setups).
   - Advantages   : fast, private, low cost, easy to control.
   - Disadvantages: centralized, less trust, single point of failure.

c) Consortium / Federated Blockchain
   - Controlled by a group of organizations together.
   - Examples: R3 (banks), Energy Web Foundation, IBM Food Trust.
   - Advantages   : faster than public, shared control, more secure than private.
   - Disadvantages: needs cooperation between members, less transparent.

d) Hybrid Blockchain
   - Mix of public and private: some data private, some public/verifiable.
   - Example: XinFin (XDC), Dragonchain.
   - Advantages   : flexible, privacy + transparency where needed.
   - Disadvantages: complex to set up, not fully transparent.

3. COMPARISON TABLE
-------------------
| Feature      | Public       | Private      | Consortium     | Hybrid        |
|--------------|--------------|--------------|----------------|---------------|
| Access       | Anyone       | One org      | Group of orgs  | Mixed         |

| Speed        | Slow         | Fast         | Fast           | Medium-Fast   |
| Privacy      | Low          | High         | High           | Configurable  |
| Example      | Bitcoin, ETH | Hyperledger  | R3 Corda       | XinFin        |

4. REAL-TIME USE CASES
----------------------
1. Cryptocurrency & payments : Bitcoin, Ethereum; fast cross-border payments (Ripple).
2. Banking & finance         : trade finance, KYC sharing between banks, DeFi lending.
3. Supply chain              : tracking products from farm to shop
                               (Walmart + IBM Food Trust tracks food origin in seconds).
4. Healthcare                : secure sharing of patient records, tracking medicines
                               to stop fake drugs.
5. Voting                    : tamper-proof, transparent digital voting.
6. Land & property records   : fraud-free ownership records (pilots in Andhra
                               Pradesh, Maharashtra).
7. Education                 : verifiable digital certificates and degrees.
8. NFTs & digital ownership  : proof of ownership for art, music, game items.
9. Insurance                 : smart contracts that auto-pay claims (e.g. flight delay).
10. Government (India)       : RBI's e-Rupee (CBDC) pilot uses blockchain-like
                               technology; Indian Blockchain Strategy by MeitY.

5. CONCLUSION
-------------
Blockchain provides a secure, transparent and tamper-proof way to store data
without a central authority. Public blockchains suit open systems like
cryptocurrency, while private, consortium and hybrid blockchains suit businesses
that need speed and privacy. Its use is growing in finance, supply chain,
healthcare and government.
